        What this model is, and what it is not

        Every number here is read from model.json, which train.py writes. Retraining updates this page; nothing on it is typed by hand.

          What it reads

          Wording only. It cannot see how long a posting has been up, whether it has been reposted, or anything about the company — which are the strongest ghost-job signals there are. It is reading the advert, not checking the job.

          SignalWeightDirection

          Where it gets things wrong

          • A well-written ghost posting. Anything with a salary, a named manager and a deadline scores low, whether or not the job exists.
          • A badly-written real job. Plenty of genuine postings are full of buzzwords and missing a salary, especially from small employers without a recruiter. Those score high and should not.
          • Short postings. Under about 80 words there is too little text for the signals to mean anything.
          • Tech-sector wording. Most labelled postings are software and office roles. Trades, healthcare, hospitality and academia phrase things differently.
          • One person's judgement. Every label reflects one reader's call, so the model inherits their blind spots along with their reasoning.
          • English only. The phrase lists are English; anything else scores as though the wording were missing.

          A heuristic, not a detector. It compares wording against a small set of hand-labelled postings and cannot know whether a job exists.
